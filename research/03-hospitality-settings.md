# Hospitality Settings — Common English Errors & Workplace Scenarios

> Real-life English for hotels, restaurants, bars, and cafés: the mistakes that cost tips, reviews, and repeat customers, plus the scenarios to practice them in.

---

## 1. Top Hospitality-Specific English Errors

### 1a. "Good Night" as a Greeting
**The problem:** Saying "Good night!" when guests arrive. In English, "good night" is ONLY for saying goodbye at the end of the evening. Using it as a greeting sounds like you're telling guests the restaurant is closing.

| Time | Correct Greeting |
|---|---|
| Before 12pm | Good morning |
| 12pm–6pm | Good afternoon |
| After 6pm | Good evening |
| Any time (safe) | Welcome / Hello |

**Only say "Good night" when guests are leaving:** "Good night! Thank you for dining with us."

### 1b. "You Want?" Instead of "Would You Like?"
The single most impactful change in hospitality English. "You want water?" sounds like an interrogation. "Would you like some water?" sounds like service.

| ❌ Too Direct | ✅ Polite | Context |
|---|---|---|
| You want water? | Would you like some water? | Taking drink order |
| You want dessert? | Would you like to see the dessert menu? | Offering dessert |
| What you want? | What would you like to order? | Taking food order |
| Give me your card. | May I take your card, please? | Payment |
| Sit here. | Please follow me. / Please have a seat here. | Seating |
| Wait. | One moment, please. / I'll be right with you. | Asking guest to wait |

### 1c. Direct Commands Instead of Polite Requests
| ❌ Command | ✅ Polite Request |
|---|---|
| Sit here | Please have a seat / Please follow me |
| Wait | One moment, please / I'll be right with you |
| Give me your card | May I take your card, please? |
| Take this menu | Here is your menu |
| Sign here | Could you sign here, please? |
| Move your car | Would you mind moving your car, please? |

**Rule:** Turn commands into questions + add "please." "Could you...?" / "Would you mind...?" / "May I...?" / "If you could just..."

### 1d. Not Repeating Orders Back
Failing to confirm an order is both a service error and a language error. Professional servers always repeat orders.

**4-Step Order Confirmation:**
1. **Signal:** "Let me confirm your order" or "So that's..."
2. **Repeat:** "So that's one steak, medium-rare, with vegetables, and one salmon, well-done, with the salad..."
3. **Check special requests:** "And no onions on the burger, correct?"
4. **Confirm done:** "Is that everything for now?"

### 1e. Not Checking Back After Serving Food
The "2-minute check-back" is a professional standard:

| Timing | Phrase |
|---|---|
| 2-3 min after serving | "How is everything?" / "Is everything okay with your meal?" / "How are you enjoying your food?" |
| Mid-meal | "Is there anything else I can get for you?" |
| If there's a problem | Fix immediately, then: "Is everything okay now?" |

### 1f. Mispronouncing Key Words
| Word | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Menu | men-OO | MEN-yoo | Stress 1st syllable |
| Dessert | DEZ-ert | deh-ZERT | Stress 2nd syllable; "DEZ-ert" = a sandy desert |
| Receipt | re-ceipt (with p) | reh-SEET | Silent "p" |
| Salmon | sal-mon | SAM-un | Silent "l" |
| Vegetable | veg-eh-TAH-bull | VEJ-tuh-bull | |
| Quinoa | quin-oh-a | KEEN-wah | |
| Gnocchi | noh-chee | NYOH-kee | |
| Bruschetta | broo-shet-ta | broo-SKET-ta | |

### 1g. Mishandling Complaints
**3-Step Complaint Formula:**
1. **Apologize (even if not your fault):** "I'm really sorry about that." / "I apologize for the inconvenience."
2. **Offer a solution immediately:** "Let me get you a fresh dish right away." / "I'll speak with the kitchen about this." / "I'll ask the manager to come speak with you."
3. **Follow up:** "Is everything okay now?" / "How is the new dish?"

**Never say:** "It's not my fault" / "I don't know" / "That's how we make it" / Argue with the customer.

### 1h. Responding to "Thank You"
| ❌ Wrong Response | ✅ Correct Response | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| It's okay. | You're welcome. / My pleasure. | "It's okay" sounds dismissive — it cost a real waiter a £15 tip |
| No problem. | You're welcome. | "No problem" is fine in casual settings but "You're welcome" or "My pleasure" is more professional |
| (silence) | You're very welcome. | Ignoring a "thank you" feels rude |

## 2. Essential Hospitality Phrases by Situation

### Hotel Front Desk

**Check-in:**
- "Welcome to [hotel name]. How may I help you?"
- "Could I have your passport, please?"
- "Please fill out this registration form."
- "Your room has been booked for three nights."
- "Your room is ready. It's on the third floor."
- "Breakfast is served from 7 to 10 a.m. in the restaurant."
- "Here is your key card. Enjoy your stay!"

**Handling requests:**
- "Certainly, let me check that for you."
- "I'm afraid that room isn't available, but I can offer you..."
- "Let me see if I can arrange that for you."
- "We offer complimentary Wi-Fi, parking, and breakfast."

**Check-out:**
- "How was your stay?"
- "Would you like a copy of your receipt?"
- "We hope to see you again soon!"
- "Is there anything else I can help you with before you go?"

**Housekeeping:**
- "Housekeeping! May I come in?"
- "I'll make up your room right away."
- "Would you like fresh towels?"
- "I'm sorry for the inconvenience. We'll fix this right away."

### Restaurant

**Greeting & seating:**
- "Good evening! Welcome to [restaurant]."
- "Do you have a reservation?"
- "Please follow me. Your table is right this way."
- "Here is your menu. May I start you off with something to drink?"

**Taking orders:**
- "Are you ready to order, or would you like a few more minutes?"
- "Would you like some water — still or sparkling?"
- "How would you like your steak cooked?"
- "Would you like any sides with that?"
- "Let me confirm your order..."

**During service:**
- "Here is your [dish]. Enjoy!"
- "How is everything? Is the food to your liking?"
- "Can I get you anything else?"
- "Would you like to see the dessert menu?"

**Payment:**
- "May I bring you the check?"
- "Would you like to pay together or separately?"
- "I'll be right back with your receipt."
- "Thank you so much. Have a wonderful evening!"

### Bar

- "What can I get for you?"
- "Would you like that on the rocks or straight?"
- "Coming right up."
- "Would you like to start a tab?"
- "Last call! We close in 15 minutes."

## 3. Hospitality Role-Play Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Difficult Check-In
**Setting:** Hotel front desk, 10pm
**Role A (Front desk agent):** Guest arrives with a reservation but the system shows no booking. The guest is tired and frustrated.
**Role B (Guest):** You've been traveling for 12 hours. You have a confirmation email but the hotel can't find it.
**Language goals:** Apologizing professionally, offering solutions, using "would" and "could" forms, staying calm under pressure.
**Key phrases:** "I apologize for the confusion." / "Let me see what I can do." / "Would you mind waiting a few minutes while I check with my manager?"

### Scenario 2: Food Allergy Question
**Setting:** Restaurant
**Role A (Server):** Customer asks if a dish contains nuts/gluten/dairy. You're not 100% sure.
**Role B (Customer):** You have a serious dairy allergy and need to confirm the sauce has no cream.
**Language goals:** Asking clarifying questions, admitting uncertainty professionally, offering to check.
**Key phrases:** "Let me double-check with the kitchen." / "I want to make sure I give you the right information." / "Would you like me to ask the chef?"

### Scenario 3: Room Complaint
**Setting:** Hotel
**Role A (Guest):** Your room's air conditioning isn't working. It's hot and you want a new room.
**Role B (Front desk agent):** The hotel is fully booked. You need to find a solution without a room change.
**Language goals:** The 3-step complaint formula, polite persistence, offering alternatives.
**Key phrases:** "I'm so sorry about that." / "Unfortunately, we're fully booked tonight, but let me..." / "I'll send maintenance up right away."

### Scenario 4: Large Party with Special Requests
**Setting:** Restaurant
**Role A (Server):** Party of 8, with various dietary restrictions, separate checks requested.
**Role B (Guest/host):** Organizing the party. Three people have dietary restrictions. You want separate checks.
**Language goals:** Confirming complex orders, managing multiple requests, remaining composed.
**Key phrases:** "Let me make sure I have this right." / "Just to confirm, the three dishes with dietary modifications are..."

### Scenario 5: Upselling at the Bar
**Setting:** Hotel bar
**Role A (Bartender):** Guest orders a basic drink. You want to suggest a premium alternative.
**Role B (Guest):** You want a gin and tonic but are open to suggestions.
**Language goals:** Polite suggestion, using "would you like" and "may I recommend" forms.
**Key phrases:** "Would you like to try our house gin?" / "May I recommend..." / "It's a bit more, but many guests prefer..."

## 4. Hospitality-Specific Error Priority List

For learners in hospitality, fix these first for maximum tip and review improvement:

1. **"You want?" → "Would you like?"** — Instant politeness upgrade
2. **"Good night" as greeting** — Prevents confusion
3. **Direct commands → Polite requests** — Prevents perceived rudeness
4. **Not confirming orders** — Prevents errors and shows professionalism
5. **"It's okay" → "You're welcome"** — Preserves the positive feeling of a "thank you"
6. **Mishandling complaints** — The 3-step formula saves tips
7. **Pronouncing menu/dessert/salmon** — Prevents confusion
8. **Not checking back after serving** — The 2-minute check-back dramatically improves perception
9. **Article errors ("You want the menu?" vs "You want a menu?")** — Subtle but noticed
10. **Over-apologizing** — Apologize once, then fix it. Multiple apologies feel unprofessional.
