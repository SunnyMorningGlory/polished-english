# Common Mistakes Catalog — Conversational English for Non-Native Professionals

> The most comprehensive category-by-category reference of errors that persist in non-native English speakers who are already proficient but make habitual mistakes in professional settings.

---

## 1. Double Comparatives & Superlatives

**The "more better" problem** — using two comparative forms together when only one is needed.

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| This is more better. | This is better. | "Better" is already the comparative of "good." Don't add "more." |
| She is more smarter. | She is smarter. | "-er" comparatives don't take "more." |
| He is more taller than me. | He is taller than I am. | Same rule; also use "than I am" not "than me" formally |
| It is more hotter today. | It is hotter today. | |
| This option is more cheaper. | This option is cheaper. | |

**Rule:** If a word already has a comparative form (good→better, bad→worse, far→farther), never add "more." For short adjectives ending in "-er" comparatives (smart→smarter, fast→faster), never add "more." Use "more" only with longer adjectives (more expensive, more important, more comfortable).

## 2. Articles (a, an, the)

The single most persistent error category for speakers of languages without article systems (Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Hindi, Slavic languages).

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| I went to store. | I went to the store. | Omitted article |
| She is nurse. | She is a nurse. | Omitted indefinite article |
| I read book. | I read a book. | Omitted article |
| I traveled to the Spain. | I traveled to Spain. | Unnecessary article before country names |
| The education is important. | Education is important. | Unnecessary "the" before uncountable noun |
| She is the best teacher in the school. | She is the best teacher in school. | (Context-dependent) |

**Rules:**
- Use **a/an** for general, non-specific nouns: "A dog ran into the park."
- Use **the** for specific nouns: "The dog ran into the park" (a particular dog).
- **No article** for: country names (most), uncountable nouns used generally, abstract nouns: "Education is important," not "The education is important."
- **Common pattern:** "I have the headache" (some languages use definite article for conditions) → "I have a headache."

## 3. Subject–Verb Agreement

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| She go to the meeting. | She goes to the meeting. | Missing third-person singular "-s" |
| One of the planes are late. | One of the planes is late. | Subject is "one," not "planes" |
| Each of the cars are fast. | Each of the cars is fast. | Subject is "each," not "cars" |
| The list of factors are important. | The list of factors is important. | Subject is "list," not "factors" |
| People is coming. | People are coming. | "People" is plural |
| The police is coming. | The police are coming. | "Police" is always plural |
| The wages is little. | The wages are little. | "Wages" is plural |

## 4. Verb Tense Errors

### 4a. Present Perfect vs. Past Simple
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I have seen him yesterday. | I saw him yesterday. |
| Did you ever been to Paris? | Have you ever been to Paris? |
| I am living here since five years. | I have been living here for five years. |
| I've been in America since 3 months. | I've been in America for 3 months. |

### 4b. "Since" vs. "For"
- **Since** = point in time: "since 2020," "since Monday," "since I was a child"
- **For** = duration: "for three years," "for two months," "for a long time"

### 4c. Present Continuous vs. Present Simple
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Tomorrow we go shopping. | Tomorrow we will go shopping. |
| I am working here since 2019. | I have been working here since 2019. / I have worked here since 2019. |

### 4d. Present Perfect Continuous confusion
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I have been knowing her for years. | I have known her for years. (stative verbs don't use continuous) |
| He has been eaten at expensive restaurants. | He has been eating at expensive restaurants. |

## 5. Preposition Errors

### 5a. Unnecessary Prepositions
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Note |
|---|---|---|
| We discussed about the results. | We discussed the results. | "Discuss" takes no preposition |
| I want to discuss about the issue. | I want to discuss the issue. | |
| He married with a French girl. | He married a French girl. | No "with" after "marry" |
| I called to him. | I called him. | No "to" needed |

### 5b. Wrong Preposition
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| She is on the car. | She is in the car. |
| I am at the Monday. | I am on Monday. |
| In January 13th, I will be twenty. | On January 13th, I will be twenty. |
| He lives house. | He lives in the house. |
| I am good in English. | I am good at English. |
| Similar than the previous study. | Similar to the previous study. |
| I am waiting him. | I am waiting for him. |

### 5c. Common Verb + Preposition Pairs (often confused)
- **Dependent on** (not "dependent of")
- **Different from** (not "different than" — though common in casual American English)
- **Interested in** (not "interested about")
- **Arrive at/in** (not "arrive to")
- **Listen to** (not "listen")
- **Look at** (not "look")
- **Think about** (not "think on")

## 6. Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| I need an advice. | I need some advice. / I need a piece of advice. | "Advice" is uncountable |
| She gave me many informations. | She gave me a lot of information. | "Information" is uncountable |
| I have many experiences. | I have a lot of experience. / I have had many experiences. | "Experience" (general) is uncountable |
| There is much dusts. | There is much dust. | Uncountable |
| There were less people. | There were fewer people. | "Fewer" for countable; "less" for uncountable |
| I have two furnitures. | I have two pieces of furniture. | "Furniture" is uncountable |

**Common uncountable nouns often miscounted:** advice, information, knowledge, research, furniture, luggage, baggage, equipment, machinery, bread, water, rice, money, evidence, progress, traffic, homework, software, vocabulary, grammar.

## 7. "Say" vs. "Tell"

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| She said me the answer. | She told me the answer. | "Tell" requires an object (who) |
| He told that he was tired. | He said that he was tired. | "Say" doesn't take a direct person object |
| Can you say me the time? | Can you tell me the time? | |
| She said to me to go. | She told me to go. | |

**Rule:** "Tell [someone] [something]" vs. "Say [something] (to someone)."

## 8. Bored vs. Boring (and -ed vs. -ing adjectives)

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| This movie is very bored. | This movie is very boring. | "-ing" = describes the thing |
| I am so boring today. | I am so bored today. | "-ed" = describes how you feel |
| The meeting was very excited. | The meeting was very exciting. | |
| I am very interesting in this. | I am very interested in this. | |
| He is very annoyed customer. | He is a very annoying customer. | |

**Other pairs:** tired/tiring, confused/confusing, surprised/surprising, disappointed/disappointing, exhausted/exhausting, frightened/frightening, shocked/shocking, disgusted/disgusting.

## 10. Word Confusion Pairs (Commonly Mixed Up)

| Word 1 | Word 2 | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Advice** (noun) | **Advise** (verb) | "Can you give me advice?" / "I advise you to..." |
| **Affect** (verb) | **Effect** (noun) | "This affects me." / "The effect was large." |
| **Borrow** (from someone) | **Lend** (to someone) | "I borrowed the book from her." / "She lent the book to me." |
| **Comfortable** (physically) | **Convenient** (suitable) | Not interchangeable |
| **Farther** (physical distance) | **Further** (degree/extent) | |
| **Lay** (put down) | **Lie** (recline) | "I will lay the paper on the table." / "I will lie down." |
| **Learn** (acquire) | **Teach** (impart) | "I will teach you" not "I will learn you" |
| **Loan** (noun) | **Lend** (verb) | "I borrowed the book" not "I loaned the book from..." |
| **Personal** (private) | **Personnel** (staff) | Pronunciation differs: PER-son-al vs. per-SON-nel |
| **Presently** (soon) | **At present** (now) | Not interchangeable |
| **Regardless** | **Irregardless** | "Irregardless" is nonstandard — never use it |
| **Can** (ability) | **May** (permission) | "I can drive" / "May I come in?" |
| **Bring** (toward you) | **Take** (away from you) | Direction-dependent |
| **Among** (3+) | **Between** (2) | "Between the two of us" / "Among all the options" |
| **Beside** (next to) | **Besides** (in addition to) | |

## 11. Pronoun Errors

### 11a. Subject vs. Object Pronouns
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Give it to I. | Give it to me. |
| Jason and me took the class. | Jason and I took the class. |
| He read as well as me. | He read as well as I (did). |

### 11b. Repeating the Subject
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| My English it is improving. | My English is improving. |
| My friend Mary she is going out with me. | My friend Mary is going out with me. |

### 11c. Possessive Pronouns
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| She lives with she's father. | She lives with her father. |
| It was John's and Peter's car. | It was John and Peter's car. (shared possession) |

| At the party, I didn't meet nobody. | At the party, I didn't meet anybody. |
| I don't know nothing about it. | I don't know anything about it. |

## 13. Question Word Order

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Where he is going? | Where is he going? | Inverted subject-verb in questions |
| Why you are late? | Why are you late? | |
| How much it costs? | How much does it cost? | |
| You want coffee? | Would you like coffee? / Do you want coffee? | Sounds like a statement without inversion |

## 14. Missing or Wrong Contractions

Using full forms where contractions are natural in spoken English:

| ❌ Unnatural | ✅ Natural |
|---|---|
| I do not know. | I don't know. |
| She is not here. | She isn't here. / She's not here. |
| I have been working. | I've been working. |
| They will be here soon. | They'll be here soon. |

And the reverse — using a contraction where full form is needed:
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I'd of come if I had a car. | I would have come if I had a car. |

---

## 15. Register Mismatch (Formal vs. Casual)

### 15a. Too Formal in Casual Settings
Using "I am desirous of" or "I am in receipt of" in conversation — sounds like a legal document, not a human.

### 15b. Too Casual in Formal Settings
Using "Hey" in emails to senior colleagues, or "What's up?" in a board meeting.

**Key balance:** Business English uses contractions but avoids slang. Casual English uses both but avoids textbook-stiff phrasing.

## 16. Business-Specific Errors

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I look forward to hear from you. | I look forward to hearing from you. | "Look forward to" + "-ing" |
| Let me presentate this. | Let me present this. | "Presentate" is not a word |
| Our headquarter is in Chicago. | Our headquarters is in Chicago. | Always plural "headquarters" |
| I have many experiences in marketing. | I have a lot of experience in marketing. | "Experience" uncountable here |
| We are five peoples in the team. | There are five people on the team. | "Peoples" is rare (only for distinct ethnic groups) |
| He is responsible of the project. | He is responsible for the project. | |
| I am working here since five years. | I have been working here for five years. | Present perfect, not present continuous |

## 18. "Would you like" vs. "You want" (Politeness)

The most impactful habit change in service industries:

| ❌ Too Direct | ✅ Polite | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| You want water? | Would you like some water? | Sounds like interrogation vs. service |
| What you want? | What would you like? | |
| Give me your card. | May I take your card, please? | |
| Sit here. | Please have a seat. | |
| Wait. | One moment, please. | |
| You want dessert? | Would you like to see the dessert menu? | |

---

## 19. Overusing Filler Words

While not strictly "wrong," these reduce perceived professional competence:
- **"Um" / "ah" / "you know"** — overuse signals uncertainty
- **"Very"** — used for everything; English has precise intensifiers ("extremely," "highly," "deeply")
- **"Actually"** — overused as a filler; can sound condescending or corrective
- **"Like"** — ubiquitous filler that erodes professionalism

**Fix:** Pause instead of filling silence. Silence is more powerful than "um."

---

## 20. Over-Apologizing

| ❌ Pattern | ✅ Better |
|---|---|
| "Sorry, sorry, I'm sorry, can I just say..." | "May I add something here?" |
| "Sorry, my English is not good..." | (Don't mention it; just speak) |
| "Sorry for my bad English..." | (Omit entirely — it draws attention to the very thing you want unnoticed) |

**Rule in professional settings:** Apologize once when appropriate, then move on. Repeated apologies make you seem less confident and draw attention to mistakes that listeners may not have noticed.

## 21. "Discuss" Without Preposition (Business-Specific)

This is one of the most common workplace errors:

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Let's discuss about the project. | Let's discuss the project. |
| We need to discuss about the budget. | We need to discuss the budget. |
| I want to discuss about this with you. | I want to discuss this with you. |

---

## 22. "Of" Instead of "Have" in Conditional

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I would of come if I had a car. | I would have come if I had a car. |
| She could of done it. | She could have done it. |
| They should of told us. | They should have told us. |

This error comes from the contraction "would've" sounding like "would of."

## 23. "Much" vs. "Many" vs. "A Lot of"

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| I have much friends. | I have many friends. / I have a lot of friends. | "Much" = uncountable, "many" = countable |
| There are much people here. | There are many people here. / There are a lot of people here. | |
| There is many water. | There is much water. / There is a lot of water. | |

---

## 24. Circumlocution (Talking Around the Subject)

Common workplace overcomplications:

| ❌ Wordy | ✅ Direct |
|---|---|
| At this point in time, I would like to... | I would like to... |
| It goes without saying that... | (just say it) |
| With reference to your question, I don't really know... | I don't know... |
| I am in possession of the documents. | I have the documents. |
| In the event that... | If... |
| Owing to the fact that... | Because... |
| At an early date... | Soon |
| In advance of the meeting... | Before the meeting |

## Summary of Priority for a Tutor App

These are the **highest-impact** errors to fix first, based on frequency and social/professional impact:

1. **Double comparatives** ("more better") — instantly marks as non-native
2. **"Would you like" vs. "You want"** — instantly sounds rude in service
3. **Article omission/addition** — the most persistent and widespread
4. **Subject-verb agreement** (third-person singular "s") — subconsciously noticed by all native speakers
5. **"Discuss about"** — extremely common in workplace settings
6. **Countable/uncountable** — "many advices," "much people"
7. **Bored vs. boring** — reverses meaning entirely
8. **"Since" vs. "for"** — present perfect tense confusion
9. **"Look forward to hearing"** — business email standard
10. **Over-apologizing** — undermines professional authority
